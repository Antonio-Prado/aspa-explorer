Questions

What is an ASPA?

An ASPA (Autonomous System Provider Authorization) is a signed object of the RPKI in which a network, an AS, lists the networks allowed to carry its routes upstream: its providers. A network with no provider lists only AS0. More in the guide

What does it protect against?

A router that checks ASPAs can refuse a route that went up through a network that is not a provider of the one before it: a route leak, and some forged paths. It adds to the ROAs, which check only who originates a prefix. The incidents seen in BGP

How do I publish one?

Where you manage your ROAs: the RPKI portal of your registry, or your own certificate authority. List every network that carries your routes upstream, and none of your peers or customers. Examples and common mistakes

Where do these numbers come from?

The ASPAs are those validated by the Routinator of this site, compared every hour with its Fort and with two rpki-client validators; the routes are those of the RIPE RIS and RouteViews collectors, and the sizes of the networks those of CAIDA. Sources and method
