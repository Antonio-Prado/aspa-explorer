What you are looking at

An ASPA (Autonomous System Provider Authorization) is a signed object published in the RPKI. In it, a customer AS lists the ASes that are allowed to act as its upstream providers. Routers that validate AS paths use these lists to spot route leaks and some forged paths.

ASPA Explorer shows every ASPA that a Routinator validator currently considers valid. Nothing here is edited or interpreted: the page reads the validator's output and presents it so you can search it and see the big picture.

At the top of the page a line summarises the data: the number of ASPAs, the time of the last validation run (always in UTC) and the version of the validator that produced it.

Looking up an AS

Type an AS number in the box at the top and press Look up. All of these are accepted: AS680, as 680 and 680. AS numbers go up to 4294967295. Anything else shows a short hint instead of a result.

The result has a header with the AS number and two outside links, bgp.tools and RIPEstat, that open in a new tab. Below it are two columns:

Its own ASPA
The providers that this AS authorizes, shown as clickable chips. For each ASPA object the page also lists the RIR (the trust anchor it comes from), its validity period (not before to not after) and the repository address of the signed object. If the AS has not published an ASPA the page says so: its upstream relationships cannot be verified.
Named as a provider
Every ASPA, from any customer, that lists this AS as a provider, with the number of such ASPAs. The list is empty for ASes that nobody names as a provider.

Long lists are cut after a few dozen chips; use +N more to expand them. Click any chip to jump to that AS.

An AS0 · no providers chip is not a link. It is the way an AS declares that it has no upstream providers at all, as networks at the top of the hierarchy do.

The overview

The four tiles

ASPAs
Customer ASes that have a valid ASPA.
Provider ASes
Distinct ASes that appear as a provider in at least one ASPA. AS0 is not counted.
Median providers
The middle value of the number of providers per ASPA, with the size of the largest provider set underneath.
No-provider ASPAs
ASPAs whose only provider is AS0.

The charts

ASPAs over time
How the ASPAs evolved day by day since 12 October 2023, the day ASPA objects started to be validated. Show chooses what is drawn: the ASPAs stacked by RIR (AFRINIC has published none so far), their total, the provider ASes, the median number of providers per ASPA, the no-provider ASPAs (AS0), or the coverage, the share of the ASes seen by the RIPE RIS route collectors that have an ASPA (one sample a week before 16 October 2026). Period narrows the chart to the last 12 months or 90 days, and the logarithmic scale, available for the single-line charts, makes a small start and a steep rise readable together. Hover over the chart, or focus it and use the left and right arrow keys (Page Up, Page Down, Home and End go further), to read a single day; Show as a table lists the last day of each month, and the daily data can be downloaded as CSV or JSON. The days up to 15 October 2026 are computed from the daily output of the RIPE NCC RPKI repository archive (opens in a new tab); from then on a daily snapshot of the validator behind this page is added. A few days with a partial or missing archive are left out, so the line may cross them in a straight stretch.
ASPAs by RIR
How many ASPAs each trust anchor (AFRINIC, APNIC, ARIN, LACNIC, RIPE NCC) validates. Each ASPA is counted once, under the trust anchor of its first source object. The percentage in the tooltip is relative to all ASPAs.
Providers per ASPA
A column chart of how many providers each customer declares, from 1 to 9 and then 10+. An ASPA with AS0 counts as one provider.
Most-named providers
The ten ASes that appear in the most ASPAs as a provider. Select a bar to look that AS up.

Hover over a bar or column, or move the keyboard focus onto it, to see the exact value in a tooltip.

The table of all ASPAs

The table lists every ASPA in order of customer AS number, with the customer, its providers and the RIR.

  • Filter by AS number shows the ASPAs where that AS is the customer or one of the providers. It needs a complete AS number; partial numbers or text are not searched, and the counter tells you so.
  • All RIRs restricts the table to one trust anchor. It combines with the AS filter.
  • The counter shows how many ASPAs match.
  • The table shows 100 rows at a time. Use Show N more under it to load the next batch. Provider sets longer than eight entries can be expanded with +N more.
  • Every AS in the table is a link to its lookup.

Checking an AS path

The Check an AS path box applies the ASPA verification procedure to a path and shows why it passes or fails. It runs in your browser, on the ASPA objects listed on the page: nothing you type is sent anywhere.

Paste the path as your router shows it, with the neighbour first and the origin last, for example 3356 1299 680 (a leading AS is optional). Then say where the route comes from:

A provider (downstream check)
The route was received from a provider. A valid path climbs from the origin through customer-to-provider hops, may have one lateral hop at the top, and then descends. The verdict combines an up-ramp (counted from the origin) and a down-ramp (counted from the neighbour).
A customer or a peer (upstream check)
The route was received from a customer or a peer. Every hop, from the origin to the neighbour, must be a customer-to-provider hop.

For each pair of adjacent ASes the table shows whether the customer's ASPA lists the other AS as a provider: lists (✓), does not list (✗), or has no ASPA (?). An AS whose ASPA contains only AS0 declares that it has no providers.

Valid
The attested hops are enough to show that the path has the expected shape.
Unknown
No hop is attested as wrong, but some ASes publish no ASPA, so the path cannot be proven valid.
Invalid
At least one hop is attested as not customer-to-provider where the procedure needs it to be: the path contains a valley, which is how a route leak or a forged path segment looks.

Limits: repeated ASes (prepending) are removed before checking; paths with an AS_SET are not accepted (the procedure treats them as Invalid); the neighbour check and route-origin validation are not part of this tool; and the result reflects the validated ASPA snapshot shown on this page, not your own router's view. The procedure follows the IETF draft BGP AS_PATH Verification Based on ASPA Objects (opens in a new tab), which is still work in progress.

Checking the routes seen in BGP

The Check the routes seen in BGP box takes a prefix (193.0.0.0/21) or an AS number (AS3333), fetches the routes that the RIPE RIS (opens in a new tab) route collectors see right now, and applies the ASPA verification to every distinct AS path. For an AS the routes are the ones it originates.

The result is a count of valid, unknown and invalid routes and a table of the distinct paths, invalid first, with the reason (the hop that is attested as wrong, or the ASes that publish no ASPA) and a link to the hop-by-hop check of that path. The downstream procedure is used, because most of the networks that feed a route collector send it their full table, as a provider does to a customer, and that includes routes they learned from their own providers (the upstream procedure would wrongly call those leaks). A path is invalid when it contains a valley: the climb from the origin and the descent from the neighbour do not meet. Paths with an AS_SET are left out. An AS that originates thousands of prefixes can have an answer too large to load: check one of its prefixes instead.

Privacy. Unlike the AS path check, this box does send what you type, the prefix or the AS number, to RIPEstat (opens in a new tab) in your browser, and receives the routes from there; the page's own server does not see it.

Recent changes and the history of an AS

Recent changes lists, for each of the last 14 days, the ASPAs that appeared (new), changed their providers (the providers added are marked +, the ones dropped −) or disappeared. The same list is available as an Atom feed. Looking up an AS adds a History block with every change of its own ASPA and the day an ASPA first named it as a provider.

The changes since 12 October 2023 were reconstructed from the daily output of the RIPE NCC RPKI repository archive (opens in a new tab); since 16 October 2026 they come from the validator behind this page, which can show a brief disappearance when a repository was unreachable. An ASPA that already existed on 12 October 2023 is shown as new on that day.

Links and keyboard use

An AS-path check has its own address too, such as https://aspa.ping6.it/#path=3356+680&from=up (from=up for a route received from a customer or a peer, from=down for one received from a provider).

Each lookup has its own address: https://aspa.ping6.it/#AS680 opens the page already showing AS680, so you can bookmark or share a result. The same works for any AS number.

Everything can be used from the keyboard: the lookup field, filters, chips, chart bars and the buttons are all reachable with Tab, and the tooltips follow the focus. The page follows your system's light or dark setting and adapts to narrow screens.

Where the data comes from

The data comes from Routinator (opens in a new tab), the RPKI validator run at rpki.ping6.it (opens in a new tab). It validates the RPKI repositories of all five RIRs and the page reads two of its outputs:

  • the list of valid ASPAs (jsonext (opens in a new tab), route origins and router keys left out), and
  • the validator status, which supplies the last validation time and the Routinator version.

The chart of ASPAs over time and the changes read three more files, history.json, events.json and changes.atom, from the history branch of the public project (opens in a new tab), where a daily job adds one row and the day's changes. The routes seen in BGP come from RIPEstat when you ask for them.

The validator refreshes about every 10 minutes and the web server keeps its answer for 2 minutes, so a new ASPA can take a little while to show up here. The last validation time in the header tells you how fresh the data is.

Reading the results

  • No ASPA is not an error. Many ASes have not published one yet. It only means the page has nothing to show for them.
  • The page shows what this validator sees. Another validator, or the same one a few minutes later, may differ slightly.
  • An ASPA lists providers for one customer. It says nothing about peers, and it does not check any specific BGP route: that is done by routers that use these objects.
  • Validity dates are those in the signed object. An expired ASPA is not shown, because the validator discards it.

If something does not load

If the page shows Could not load the ASPA data, the validator is probably restarting. Wait a minute and reload. If it persists, the error text in the message shows which request failed.

Accessibility

ASPA Explorer is built to be usable by everyone, including people who use a keyboard, a screen reader, magnification or high-contrast settings. The target is conformance with the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 (opens in a new tab) at level AA. The pages have been checked with the axe-core automated testing tool in both the light and the dark theme, with no issues reported. They have not been audited by an independent body or tested with screen-reader users, so there may be gaps that an automated tool cannot find.

What is in place

  • Keyboard. Every control can be reached and used with Tab, Shift+Tab, Enter and Space. The focused element has a visible outline. A Skip to main content link appears first when you press Tab.
  • Structure. Pages use landmarks (header, main, footer) and a single heading hierarchy. The table has a caption and column headers, and every form field has a label.
  • Charts. Each bar or column, and the chart of ASPAs over time, can take keyboard focus, carries its label and value as text, and shows its tooltip on focus as well as on hover. The exact numbers are also in the table (for the time chart, a table of month ends) and in the lookup results, so no information depends on seeing the chart.
  • Colour. Text and controls meet the WCAG AA contrast ratio in the light and the dark theme, which follow your system setting. Colour is never the only way something is shown.
  • Screen readers. Lookup and path-check results are announced when they appear. Links in the footer open in a new tab and say so in their accessible name. Decorative images are hidden from assistive technology and meaningful ones have a text alternative.
  • Zoom and small screens. The layout reflows without horizontal scrolling down to a width of 320 pixels, which is what a desktop window shows at 400% zoom.
  • Motion. If your system asks for reduced motion, the page does not animate scrolling.

Known limitations

  • The tables and chips can be long: a large provider set or the 100-row table pages are tiring to read with a screen reader. Use the lookup or the AS filter to narrow them down.
  • The outside links (bgp.tools, RIPEstat) and the validator's raw JSON are not under this project's control.

Feedback

If you meet a barrier, please tell us: open an issue in the project repository, or write to antonio.prado@dei.unipd.it saying which page, what you were trying to do, and which browser and assistive technology you use. Reports are read and answered.

About this page

ASPA Explorer is a small static page with no external dependencies and no cookies: your lookups happen in your browser and are not sent anywhere. The only thing this site learns about you is what any web server learns from a request; the footer shows the address your request came from, as the server sees it, and links it to RIPEstat; nothing is sent to RIPEstat unless you follow the link. The footer links to the project on the DEI GitLab and shows the release you are using and the commit it was built from.

The source code, the changelog and instructions to report a problem are in the project repository.
